# Product

## Register

product

## Users

University students across the full day: morning rush before lectures, afternoon between classes, evening part-time shifts, late-night campus returns. They open the app with a destination in mind and a tight window to act. Phone in hand, one eye on the clock. Both sides of the ride: the same student who hitches a ride Monday might host one Tuesday.

## Product Purpose

Noru is a peer ride-sharing app for students at a single institution. It matches riders and drivers within a closed, verified community, cutting the cost and friction of getting to transit. University Entra login and mandatory face capture aren't onboarding steps — they're the product: the reason riders trust the person behind the wheel. Success means a student saves money and catches their bus without thinking hard about either.

## Brand Personality

Trusted, fast, familiar. Feels like borrowing a friend's car, not hailing a cab. Warm without being loud. Confident without being corporate.

## Anti-references

- **Uber / Lyft**: map-centric, commercial-feeling, impersonal. Noru is not a marketplace; it's a community. Avoid anything that signals "gig economy."
- **Generic SaaS**: SaaS-cream whites, teal accents, hollow dashboard grids. This is a human-scale mobile product.

## Design Principles

1. **Peers, not strangers.** The trust mechanism is the product. University accounts and face verification are features, not friction — surface them as the reason this app exists.
2. **Fast above all.** A student at a bus stop doesn't browse. Every primary action must land in one or two taps. Never bury the ride list or the "Host" button.
3. **Quietly confident.** No alerts, no hype, no push. The interface knows the job and gets out of the way. Information density serves the user, not the app's ego.
4. **Safety as presence, not noise.** Safety isn't a banner or a modal. It's built into who can even open the app. Never make it feel like a warning system.
5. **Earned familiarity.** The app should feel like it knows your habits over time. Habit-forming without being manipulative — no dark patterns, no artificial urgency.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No hard requirements established yet. Ship first. Ensure tap targets meet platform minimums (44pt) and text contrast is readable on both the dark and light themes.
